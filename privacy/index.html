Privacy

Applies to every Juris Games app: Tasks, Sobres and QR. Last updated 21 November 2026.

We don't collect anything, unless you choose to send us feedback (below). There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no trackers. We can't read your lists or budgets or your name, and we don't record who you share with. (Our mailbox, below, sees which network addresses use the same encrypted mailbox, like any server would; it keeps no log of them.)

Where your data lives

Your lists (Tasks) and budgets (Sobres: envelopes, accounts, amounts, what you spent on, notes and who entered each entry) are stored on your own devices. When you share one, or use the app on more than one device, changes travel between those devices end-to-end encrypted: only the devices in it hold the keys. An invite (its link or its 8 words) carries the key itself: until it expires (at most 30 days) or is cancelled, anyone who sees it can read the list or budget, so share it only with the people you invite.

To reach devices that aren't online at the same time, the encrypted changes are left for a while with public relays and with other people's copies of the app that help carry them (Nostr relays, which keep them as long as each relay decides; the Mainline DHT; iroh relays; and a small mailbox we run at mailbox.jurisgames.com, which every shared list's encrypted changes go to and which deletes them 14 days after they were last refreshed). In Tasks, pictures and long notes attached to tasks travel the same ways and are also left, encrypted the same way, on public Blossom file servers. To find each other directly, your devices leave their current network address on the Mainline DHT, sealed so only the members can read it. None of them can read the contents. Like any internet service, they can see the network address that connects to them, when and how much. The devices of the people you share with learn your devices' network addresses too, so they can connect directly.

What your device does for others

"Help others sync" (in Settings) is on at first: your device keeps small amounts of other people's encrypted changes for up to 14 days and passes them on, so their devices find each other when they aren't online at the same time. It can't read them, and those people's devices see your device's network address. On a phone it does this only on Wi-Fi while charging; on a computer whenever the app runs. On a computer "Relay connections for others" is also on at first: it passes on other people's encrypted connections and, if your router allows it, asks the router to open a port for that. Turn either off in Settings.

The app checks for a newer version by reading a public file from apps.jurisgames.com, or from GitHub if that doesn't answer. Nothing about you is in that request.

Sobres never connects to your bank and never asks for bank logins: you type in what you spend and receive.

Permissions

Tasks: notifications (changes in shared lists, reminders), exact alarms (reminders on time), camera only if you scan a QR code, and pictures you choose to attach. Sobres: notifications (someone joins a budget you share, or your own join finishes) and camera only if you scan a QR code. On Google Play builds of Tasks and Sobres, Google's scanner reads QR codes without the camera permission. QR: camera, to scan (on every build, Google Play included).

Backups

On Android, a backup file can be included in your device's own backup (Google's, if you use it); on a computer a copy is kept each day in the app's folder. The file is packed, not protected, unless you set a backup password (Settings > Backup password; Tasks 1.0.11 and Sobres 1.0.3 and later). Without one, the key that opens it is inside it, so whoever gets the file can read your lists or budgets: keep it like you would the lists themselves. With one, the file opens only with that password. The app keeps a key made from it on the device, never in the backup, so daily backups don't ask, and a forgotten password can't be recovered.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app deletes what it keeps on that device, with two exceptions for Tasks and Sobres: on Android, the backup file in your device's own backup (if you use one) stays there and comes back if you install the app again; on a computer, the app's folder (on Windows %APPDATA%\JurisTasks or %APPDATA%\JurisSobres) stays until you delete it. Deleting a shared list or budget removes it for everyone in it.

QR

QR reads codes on the phone itself: what the camera sees and the pictures you choose are never sent anywhere. The camera is used only while the scan screen is open. Codes you make are drawn on the phone and go through no server. The history (what each code said, its kind and when) is kept only in the app's own storage on the phone and is left out of device backups; turn it off or clear it in History. The app connects to the internet only to send feedback, and only when you press Send. A link in a code opens only when you tap Open.

Feedback

"Send feedback" (in the menu) sends what you write, its kind (a problem, an idea, something else), the app's version and language and, only if you type it, your email, to feedback.jurisgames.com, which emails it to hello@jurisgames.com and stores nothing itself. With "Include technical details" (ticked at first; untick it to leave them out, and "See what's sent" shows everything before it goes) it also sends your system and the app's diagnostics: the state of each sync layer, relay addresses and how often they worked, storage sizes (one line per shared list or budget, numbered, never named) and the recent sync log. Never your lists, tasks, budgets, amounts, names or who you share with. The email stays in our inbox as long as it takes to answer and fix things. To stop floods the server counts reports per network per day under a one-way code of the network address (never the address itself), deleted the next day.
