Privacy

Applies to every Juris Games app, including Tasks. Last updated 18 November 2026.

We don't collect anything, unless you choose to send us feedback (below). There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no trackers. We never see your lists, your name or who you share with.

Where your data lives

Your lists are stored on your own devices. When you share a list, or use the app on more than one device, changes travel between those devices end-to-end encrypted: only the devices in that list hold the keys.

To reach devices that aren't online at the same time, the encrypted changes are left for a while with public relays and with other people's copies of the app that help carry them (Nostr relays, the Mainline DHT, iroh relays, and a small mailbox we run at mailbox.jurisgames.com). None of them can read the contents. Like any internet service, they can see the network address that connects to them.

Permissions

Notifications (changes in shared lists, reminders), exact alarms (reminders on time), camera only if you scan a QR code (on Google Play builds, Google's scanner does this without the camera permission), and pictures you choose to attach.

Backups

On Android, a sealed backup file can be included in your device's own backup (Google's, if you use it). It is encrypted before it leaves the app.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app deletes everything on that device. Deleting a shared list removes it for everyone in it.

Feedback

"Send feedback" (in Settings) sends what you write, its kind (a problem, an idea, something else), the app's version and language and, only if you type it, your email, to feedback.jurisgames.com, which emails it to hello@jurisgames.com and stores nothing itself. With "Include technical details" (ticked at first; untick it to leave them out, and "See what's sent" shows everything before it goes) it also sends your system and the app's diagnostics: the state of each sync layer, relay addresses and how often they worked, storage sizes and the recent sync log. Never your lists, tasks, names or who you share with. The email stays in our inbox as long as it takes to answer and fix things. To stop floods the server counts reports per network per day under a one-way code of the network address (never the address itself), deleted the next day.
