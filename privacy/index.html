Privacy

Applies to every Juris Games app: Tasks, Sobres, Ciclo, Cada, QR, Papeles and Toma. Last updated 27 November 2026.

We don't collect anything, unless you choose to send us feedback (below). There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no trackers. We can't read your lists, budgets or cycle journal or your name, and we don't record who you share with. (Our mailbox, below, sees which network addresses use the same encrypted mailbox, like any server would; it keeps no log of them.)

Where your data lives

Your lists (Tasks), budgets (Sobres: envelopes, accounts, amounts, what you spent on, notes and who entered each entry) and homes (Cada: the things you do or buy, their rooms and how often, each day one was done or bought, how much, notes and who logged it) are stored on your own devices. When you share one, or use the app on more than one device, changes travel between those devices end-to-end encrypted: only the devices in it hold the keys. An invite (its link or its 8 words) lets whoever has it ask to join: until it expires (at most 30 days) or is cancelled, the first member device that is online lets them in, without anyone having to approve, and from then on they can read the list or budget. Share it only with the people you invite, and check the members after someone joins. (Invites from older versions carried the key itself; the same advice applies.)

To reach devices that aren't online at the same time, the encrypted changes are left for a while with public relays and with other people's copies of the app that help carry them (Nostr relays, which keep them as long as each relay decides; the Mainline DHT; iroh relays; and a small mailbox we run at mailbox.jurisgames.com, which every shared list's encrypted changes go to and which deletes them 14 days after they were last refreshed). In Tasks, pictures and long notes attached to tasks travel the same ways and are also left, encrypted the same way, on public Blossom file servers. To find each other directly, your devices leave their current network address on the Mainline DHT, sealed so only the members can read it. None of them can read the contents. Like any internet service, they can see the network address that connects to them, when and how much. The devices of the people you share with learn your devices' network addresses too, so they can connect directly.

What your device does for others

"Help others sync" (in Settings) is on at first: your device keeps small amounts of other people's encrypted changes for up to 14 days and passes them on, so their devices find each other when they aren't online at the same time. It can't read them, and those people's devices see your device's network address. On a phone it does this only on Wi-Fi while charging; on a computer whenever the app runs. On a computer "Relay connections for others" is also on at first: it passes on other people's encrypted connections and, if your router allows it, asks the router to open a port for that. Turn either off in Settings.

The app checks for a newer version by reading a public file from apps.jurisgames.com, or from GitHub if that doesn't answer. It also reads a second public, signed file from the same places: the network settings (which relays, helpers and mailbox the apps use). Nothing about you is in those requests.

Sobres never connects to your bank and never asks for bank logins: you type in what you spend and receive.

Ciclo (your cycle journal)

What you log in Ciclo (periods, flow, symptoms, moods, sex, tests, medicine, temperature, weight, notes, birth control and your settings) is health data, and it stays on your own devices. Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else. Predictions, patterns and the report are worked out on the device. When you link another device or keep a backup, the journal travels and is kept as described above, end-to-end encrypted.

Your journal is never shared with a partner. "Share with a partner" makes a separate small summary that holds only the items you tick (your next period and cycle day, your fertile window, today's feelings and energy, your pregnancy week and due date) and your name, and shares that summary like a shared list: your partner's devices can read it, and its encrypted changes travel the same ways. "Stop sharing" removes it from your device; the copy on your partner's devices stops getting updates.

"Report for a doctor" hands the page to your device's print service (Android's, or the computer's print dialog); where it goes from there (a printer, a PDF) is your choice. "Export CSV" makes a file you choose where to send. Reminders are made on the device and say only "Ciclo reminder" on the lock screen. The lock uses your device's own screen lock; Ciclo never sees your PIN, pattern or fingerprint.

Permissions

Tasks: notifications (changes in shared lists, reminders), exact alarms (reminders on time), camera only if you scan a QR code, and pictures you choose to attach. Sobres: notifications (someone joins a budget you share, or your own join finishes) and camera only if you scan a QR code. Ciclo: notifications (reminders, and joins as for Sobres), starting at boot (to set your reminders again after a restart) and camera only if you scan a QR code. Cada: notifications (the daily reminder, only if you turn it on; someone logs something in a home you share; joins as for Sobres), starting at boot (to set the reminder again after a restart) and camera only if you scan a QR code. The reminder is made on the device and says only "Cada reminder" on the lock screen. On Google Play builds of Tasks, Sobres, Ciclo and Cada, Google's scanner reads QR codes without the camera permission. QR: camera, to scan (on every build, Google Play included).

Backups

On Android, a backup file can be included in your device's own backup (Google's, if you use it); on a computer a copy is kept each day in the app's folder. The file is packed, not protected, unless you set a backup password (Settings > Backup password; Tasks 1.0.11 and Sobres 1.0.3 and later). Without one, the key that opens it is inside it, so whoever gets the file can read your lists, budgets or journal: keep it like you would the lists themselves. With one, the file opens only with that password. The app keeps a key made from it on the device, never in the backup, so daily backups don't ask, and a forgotten password can't be recovered.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app deletes what it keeps on that device, with two exceptions for Tasks, Sobres, Ciclo and Cada: on Android, the backup file in your device's own backup (if you use one) stays there and comes back if you install the app again; on a computer, the app's folder (on Windows %APPDATA%\JurisTasks, %APPDATA%\JurisSobres, %APPDATA%\JurisCiclo or %APPDATA%\JurisCada) stays until you delete it. Deleting a shared list, budget or home removes it for everyone in it.

QR

QR reads codes on the phone itself: what the camera sees and the pictures you choose are never sent anywhere. The camera is used only while the scan screen is open. Codes you make are drawn on the phone and go through no server. The history (what each code said, its kind and when) is kept only in the app's own storage on the phone and is left out of device backups; turn it off or clear it in History. The app connects to the internet only to send feedback, and only when you press Send. A link in a code opens only when you tap Open.

Papeles

Papeles works on your PDFs on the phone or computer itself: reading, searching, filling forms, signing, marking up, scanning, joining and shrinking all happen on the device, and no file, page, photo or text you type is sent anywhere. It has no servers. A PDF you save goes where you choose; the file you opened is never changed.

Your drawn signature is kept only in the app's own storage on the device, to place it again next time; "Draw a new signature" replaces it. It is a picture of your signature, not a digital signature. The list of recent files keeps their names and how to open them again, also only in the app's own storage. On Android nothing of Papeles goes into your device's backup or into a transfer to a new phone. The scanner uses your phone's own camera app, which hands the photo straight back to Papeles: Papeles has no camera permission and keeps the photo only until the page is made. The app connects to the internet only to send feedback, and only when you press Send; its technical details are the app's version, your system and whether the PDF reader started, never your files or their names.

Toma

Toma edits your videos on the phone or computer itself: cutting, joining, speed, text, music, the preview and the export all happen on the device, and no video, photo, sound or text you type is sent anywhere. It has no servers. On a phone it reads the videos, photos and sounds you pick from where they already are (it keeps permission to read those you picked, so your project opens again) and saves the finished video to your gallery, in Movies/Toma; on a computer it saves where you choose. Your project (which files it uses, the cuts, texts and settings) is kept only in the app's own storage. The app connects to the internet only to send feedback, and only when you press Send; its technical details are the app's version, your system and how it makes videos (on a computer, which video encoder it uses), never your videos, photos or their names.

If a page of the site fails to load, your browser may report that failure (the address it tried and the kind of error) to Cloudflare, which serves the site.

Feedback

"Send feedback" (in the menu) sends what you write, its kind (a problem, an idea, something else), the app's version and language and, only if you type it, your email, to feedback.jurisgames.com, which emails it to hello@jurisgames.com and stores nothing itself. With "Include technical details" (ticked at first; untick it to leave them out, and "See what's sent" shows everything before it goes) it also sends your system and the app's diagnostics: the state of each sync layer, relay addresses and how often they worked, storage sizes (one line per shared list or budget, numbered, never named) and the recent sync log (in QR, only whether its history is on and how many codes it holds, never what the codes say). Never your lists, codes, tasks, budgets, amounts, cycle journal, names or who you share with. The email stays in our inbox as long as it takes to answer and fix things. To stop floods the server counts reports per network per day under a one-way code of the network address (never the address itself), deleted the next day.
